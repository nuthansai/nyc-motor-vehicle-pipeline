Date range: 1 year
    Local pipeline: 2 months

 ---- Risk questions (these answer "does weather change risk?")---

1.Crash rate by weather condition and hour of day: crashes per weather-hour for each condition (dry, rain, snow, and so on), split by hour of day. Comparing rain vs dry at the same hour of day removes rush hour as a confound.
2.Crash rate by precipitation intensity: crashes per weather-hour across bands of precipitation (none, light, moderate, heavy). It shows whether risk rises steadily with intensity or jumps at a threshold.
3.Weather transitions: crash rate in the first hours after rain or snow begins, compared with later hours of the same event. This tests the idea that sudden changes catch drivers off guard.
4.Temperature near freezing: crash rate by temperature band, with a close look at the band around 0°C, combined with precipitation.

------Cause and outcome questions-------
5.Contributing factors by weather condition: which contributing factors rise in which conditions (for example, factors related to slippery roads vs driver inattention). This links the weather data to the reasons officers recorded.
6.Severity by weather: injury-or-fatal collision rate by weather condition, with counts shown beside the percentages.
7.Who gets hurt: if the injury and death fields are split by pedestrians, cyclists, and motorists, show how that mix changes with weather.

----Place questions------
8.By borough: how the weather effect differs across boroughs, with the caveat that one city-wide weather point makes every borough share the same weather.
